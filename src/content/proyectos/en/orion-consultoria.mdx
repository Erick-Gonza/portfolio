## The problem

Orion is my consultancy: I design and deliver AI solutions built for each business, with at most one week from diagnosis to delivery. I needed a page that explained that offer to business owners without empty promises or made-up numbers, and that led to a single step: booking a free diagnostic call.

## The solution

The page follows a sales script from top to bottom: the concrete promise (custom AI, ready in 7 days) with the project week as the visual; the cost of waiting, with a calculator where visitors enter their own hours and rates; a manifesto that lights up word by word as you scroll; the process day by day; example solutions; a comparison with traditional consulting; an FAQ that answers objections; and the same call to action at the end.

The content rule is to make nothing up: the calculator uses the visitor's numbers and says it isn't a savings promise, and the testimonials section stays unpublished until there are real reviews.

## How it's built

It starts from my Astro landing template, adapted to this offer.

- All content lives in `src/data/sitio.ts`; sections, meta tags, JSON-LD and `llms.txt` read from it. An empty field never reaches the build and is flagged as pending in development.
- SEO: JSON-LD with `ProfessionalService`, `WebSite`, `WebPage` and `FAQPage`, a sitemap, `robots.txt` and `llms.txt` for AI assistants.
- Light and dark themes with CSS tokens, no flash on load, and the Geist typeface served from the site's own domain.
- Lenis for smooth scrolling synced with GSAP ScrollTrigger; reveal animations use IntersectionObserver instead of scroll listeners.
- A static build served by nginx in Docker, deployed on Dokploy on every push.

## Technical challenges

- **A canvas galaxy that stays in the background.** The backdrop is a 2D canvas with three star populations (scattered field, spiral arms and fine dust that draws the shape), with differential rotation (the core spins faster than the edges), twinkling and depth-based parallax. The star count scales with screen area, with caps; stars are generated in relative units so the mobile browser bar resizing the viewport doesn't reshuffle them; and colors come from CSS variables so they follow the theme.
- **Respecting people who don't want motion.** With `prefers-reduced-motion` the galaxy renders a single still frame, Lenis stays off and the manifesto shows up fully lit.
- **An honest calculator.** It validates inline, shows the error next to the field and keeps the last valid result instead of showing `NaN`.
