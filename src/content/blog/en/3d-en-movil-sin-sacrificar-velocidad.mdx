The home page of this site has a low-poly planet with an asteroid ring and a ship that orbits and reacts to your cursor (or to the gyroscope, if you open it on your phone). It looks good, but a 3D hero carries an obvious risk: the page loads slowly, the phone heats up, and the visitor leaves before reading a single line.

These are the rules I followed so that doesn't happen.

## 1. Text never waits for 3D

The home page HTML is prerendered by Astro and doesn't depend on JavaScript. Behind the headline there's an **SVG poster**: a gradient hexagon and a ring, under 1 kB, that shows up instantly.

three.js loads later, as a separate chunk, once the browser has finished painting and is idle (`requestIdleCallback`). When the scene is ready, the canvas fades in and the poster fades out.

## 2. Decide before downloading

Before importing three.js I check whether it's worth it:

- `prefers-reduced-motion`: if the person asked for less motion, the poster stays.
- Data saver mode (`navigator.connection.saveData`).
- Very limited devices: low memory (`deviceMemory`) or few cores.
- WebGL 2 support.

If any check fails, the visitor sees the poster and the page works the same. Nobody downloads 150 kB of 3D engine they won't use.

## 3. Less of everything on mobile

On small screens the scene is the same, just cheaper:

- The planet uses one less subdivision level (roughly half the faces).
- Fewer stars and fewer asteroids.
- `devicePixelRatio` capped at 1.5 and no antialiasing: on a high-density screen you barely notice, and the GPU thanks you.
- The asteroids are an `InstancedMesh`, so the whole ring is drawn in a single call.

## 4. Don't draw what isn't visible

The render loop stops when the hero scrolls out of view (`IntersectionObserver`) or when the tab goes to the background (`visibilitychange`). If you scroll down to read the projects, the GPU gets a break.

## 5. The gyroscope on iOS needs permission

On Android the `deviceorientation` event just arrives. On iOS you have to ask for permission from a user gesture, so the request happens on the first tap on the hero. If the person says no, the scene keeps animating on its own.

## What's next

Right now the scene uses geometry generated in code. The next step is swapping it for low-poly models made in Blender and exported to GLB. The scene already looks for those objects by name (`Planeta`, `Luna`, `Nave`, `Asteroide`), so the swap will be just dropping in a file.
